# Messaging

Three kinds of conversation:

- **Case team** — a thread on a matter, among the people on it.
- **Client channel** — the one thread a matter can have with its client.
- **Direct message** — person to person at the firm.

**Inbox** collects all of them. A matter's **Messages** tab shows only its own.

## Starting a thread

**New thread** on a matter asks for a subject, an audience and a first message.

**Who's in it** — you are added automatically; uncheck anyone it is not for.
Only people already on the matter can be added.

## The client channel

A client channel is **created shut**. The client cannot see it until the **lead
attorney** opens it with **Open to client**.

Until then it shows *not yet open to the client*, and the dashboard marks it
*Unreleased*.

A matter has at most one client channel. If one already exists, you are pointed
at it.

The client's own seats are added automatically; everyone else is your choice.
The client's seat is opened and closed by release, not from the thread roster.

<Callout type="warning">
  Messaging is a plan capability. On a plan without it, case threads and direct
  messages are unavailable — see [Plans and limits](/reference/limits).
</Callout>

## Files are not attachments

Dropping a file into the composer does not attach it to the thread.

<Callout type="info">
  Files live on the matter, not in a thread. File it under **Documents**, then
  **cite** it here.
</Callout>

**Cite** searches the matter's documents and tasks and links one into the
message. A citation the reader is not allowed to see shows as *Withheld* rather
than leaking its title.

## Mentions

Type `@` to name someone on the thread. They get a notification — *… named you
in a conversation*. Only people on the thread match.

## Roster

**In this thread** lists who is in it. Add anyone on the matter; remove anyone
who should not be. Additions and removals are written into the thread itself as
system lines.

## Read receipts

**Who has seen this** shows, per message, who has read it and who has not.

## Closed matters

*This matter is closed. The history stays; the composer does not.*
