# Duplicates and relations

Detection compares documents as they arrive and proposes pairs. **Nothing is
merged either way** — a person decides.

<Callout type="info">
  Auto-merging a near-duplicate is how the one copy carrying a handwritten note
  in the margin disappears. Detection proposes; a person confirms.
</Callout>

## Answering a proposal

A banner on the documents tab counts the pairs awaiting a decision. Opening it
shows each pair with a similarity percentage where one could be computed.

Two answers:

- **Same document** — recorded as the same document.
- **Different** — recorded as two different documents.

Either way the decision is stored with your name and the time, and nothing is
merged or deleted. Answered pairs move to *Already decided*.

## The relations a pair can carry

| Relation | Inverse |
| --- | --- |
| Duplicate of | Duplicate of |
| Near-duplicate of | Near-duplicate of |
| Supersedes | Superseded by |
| Attachment of | Has attachment |
| Exhibit of | Has exhibit |
| Responds to | Answered by |
| Excerpt of | Excerpted in |

They appear under **Related documents** on the detail sheet, from both sides.

## Versions

A document can carry several versions — *v2 — current*, *v1 — superseded* — each
recording who filed it. The current version is what the viewer opens and what
the analysis reads.

## Arrivals versus duplicates

These are different things.

An **arrival** is the same bytes reaching the firm again — from another
custodian, or by another route. Filing them records another arrival on the
existing document rather than creating a second one. One stored object, several
rows under *Where it came from*.

A **duplicate** is two documents whose text is similar enough that they may be
the same thing. That is a judgement, and it is yours.
