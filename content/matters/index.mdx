# Opening a matter

**New case** starts a four-step intake. It is saved as a draft the moment you
continue past step 1, and only you can see your own drafts — they appear on the
case list under *Your unfinished intakes*. A draft is a lead, not a case.

Only a licensed attorney or the firm owner can take a matter on.

## Step 1 — The matter

## Step 2 — Parties and conflicts

Covered in full on [Parties and conflicts](/matters/parties). In short: the
conflict check has nothing to search until at least the client is attached, and
it re-runs every time a party is added or removed.

## Step 3 — Deadline and lead

The two fields the database will not let an active matter go without.

**Statute deadline** — the final date by which the firm must file. No override,
no TBD. A missed statute of limitations kills the claim permanently.

**Lead attorney** — only members holding a bar number appear in this list. The
same licence is what grants the right to approve an AI run later.

<Callout type="info">
  Not in the list yourself? If your bar number is not on file, record it and the
  state that issued it on **Your account** and you will be taken straight back
  to this step. If you are not an attorney or firm owner, you cannot lead a
  matter at all — ask an attorney to take it on.
</Callout>

## Step 4 — Review and activate

The review step shows everything you entered, who will be able to open the
matter, the retention it inherits from the firm profile, and exactly what
pressing **Activate** writes:

<Steps>

### The row flips to active

With your id on it permanently as the creator. That does not change if you later
leave the matter, or the firm.

### The parties and their roles are kept

A party the firm already knows keeps its history; a new one starts its own.

### The conflict result and your reason are kept

Kept whether it came back clear or not, so the decision to proceed is as
recoverable as the decision not to.

### The access rows are computed

Two, to begin with — you and the lead. Every later grant, wall or role change
recomputes them rather than being read on the fly at request time.

</Steps>

## The three blockers

A draft cannot go active until all three are satisfied. The review step tracks
them as *Still outstanding*.

1. **A statute deadline.**
2. **A lead attorney.**
3. **A cleared conflict check** — run, and either clear or cleared in writing by
   an attorney.

The first two are constraints on the row itself: an active matter without them
simply fails to save, whatever the screen does. The third is the product's own —
a firm can be made to record a decision, and cannot be made to make a good one.

## Who can open it once it is live

**You** — as creator: write, and manage the team.

**The lead** — approve, release, and manage the team.

**Everyone else at the firm** — no access row, so no trace. Not in counts, not
in search.

<Callout type="info">
  Being firm owner is not access to a matter. It is a ceiling on what you could
  do once somebody puts you on one.
</Callout>

## Discarding a draft

**Discard draft** on the review step removes it. Nothing was ever live, so
nothing is retained.
