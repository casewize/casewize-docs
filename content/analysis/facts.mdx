# Your facts

The analysis reads documents. Some of what an attorney knows was never written
down — a call, a concession made in a corridor, a client's account of something
no record captures.

**Your facts**, on the Analysis tab, is where that goes. State one fact, as you
would to a colleague.

## What recording a fact does

A fact you record is given to **every later analysis**. Adding one re-runs the
analysis, so the effect is immediate rather than waiting for the next document.

## Locking a fact

A **locked** fact is treated as **settled** rather than as something to re-test
against a document that appears to disagree with it.

<Callout type="info">
  This is the difference that matters. An unlocked fact is one more input the
  engine weighs. A locked one is ground truth: if a document contradicts it, the
  document is the thing in question, not your fact.
</Callout>

Lock on the way in with **Lock as verified**, or lock and unlock afterwards.

## Excluding a fact

A fact can be marked **excluded** — kept on the record, not given to runs. Use it
for something that turned out to be wrong, where deleting it would lose the
history of having believed it.

**Remove** deletes it outright.

## Facts versus the chronology

They are different tools.

- A **fact** is a proposition. It has no date and is handed to every stage of
  every run.
- A **chronology event** is something that happened on a date. Adding one by
  hand is for what no document records — the call, the meeting with no minutes.

See [Chronology](/chronology).
